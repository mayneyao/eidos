**Versions** is Eidos Lite's built-in local time machine for your entire Space. It captures immutable snapshots of your `.eidos` tables, Markdown notes, and assets at key milestones.

All version data is stored strictly on your local disk. **No account sign-in or internet connection is required.**

---

## 1. Enable Versioning

1. Open any Space in Eidos Lite.
2. Click the **Versions** button (clock icon) in the top-right corner.
3. Click **Enable versioning**.

Eidos Lite immediately captures the initial state of your entire Space as its first snapshot. Subsequently, opening the Versions panel presents two primary tabs: **Changes** and **History**.

---

## 2. Saving a Version Snapshot

Whenever you complete a meaningful milestone (e.g. "Finalized August client invoices" or "Completed project brief draft"):

1. Click the clock icon in the top right.
2. In the **Changes** list, review the files that have been created, modified, or deleted.
3. In the **Version note** field at the bottom, type a concise summary (e.g. "Release ready draft").
4. Click **Save version**.

The changes are instantly committed to your timeline in **History**, ready to be referenced whenever needed.

---

## 3. Reviewing Historical Diffs

1. Open the Versions panel and switch to the **History** tab.
2. Browse the chronological timeline showing commit timestamps and notes.
3. Click any milestone to see which files changed.
4. Click on an individual file to inspect visual side-by-side diffs for tables and text documents.
5. Browsing historical changes never modifies your current working files.

---

## 4. One-Click Space Rollback

If recent edits were a mistake or you accidentally deleted important files, you can restore your whole Space to any prior point in time:

1. Open the **History** tab and select the version you wish to restore.
2. Click **Restore this version**.
3. Confirm by clicking **Restore Space** in the confirmation dialog.

Eidos Lite instantly restores all files in your folder to that exact milestone. For maximum safety, the system automatically creates a safeguard snapshot of your current state just before rolling back, meaning **you can even undo the restoration if you change your mind**.

---

## 5. Excluding Large or Temporary Files

If your Space contains large cache directories, build outputs, or dependencies (like `node_modules`) that shouldn't inflate version history, create a `.gitignore` or `.graftignore` file in your Space root:

```text
node_modules/
*.tmp
cache/
```

Files matching these patterns will be ignored in the Changes tab and kept out of your historical snapshots.
